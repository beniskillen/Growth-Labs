THE CHALLENGE

High velocity hides a very ordinary leak.

Exchanges compete for the same creators, the same VIP referrers, the same affiliate networks. The one that wins is rarely the one that shouted loudest that week. It is the one the community leader can explain in a sentence.

Without that sentence, business development decays into favours and follow-ups. The pipeline is a set of chats. Nothing compounds.

THE PROCESS

How the category actually got built

This is a client engagement. I did not run the exchange. I installed the partner system around KOL, affiliate, VIP and institutional business development.

  1. 01
    Step 01 — Get clear on what’s really going on

    The chase was the constraint

    Busy is not a system. I split the partners until a VIP conversation and a creator conversation stopped pretending to be the same sale.

  2. 02
    Step 02 — Give the category a mechanism

    Authority, not a link

    The thing a community leader can use is status and a clean path for their people. That became the offer. Not “please add our link.”

  3. 03
    The machine

    Playbooks, proposals, one CRM

    Every segment got a way to be worked. The proposal stopped being a personal art project. The CRM became the place a deal lived, so a week of work did not vanish when someone travelled.

  4. 04
    The cadence

    Fifty outreaches a week, on purpose

    Cold and warm, plus the rooms where the relationship already had heat. The number is the cadence. It is how you know the system is on. It is not a trophy for its own sake.

  5. THEN
    Then

    Default, not desperate

    When the onboarding path holds, you stop restarting the category every Monday. That is the whole point in a market that goes stale in a week.
