04 / DEFINE THE WORDS

Marketing is every piece of communication a business has with its market.

Prospects and customers. The ad, the site, the email, the invoice, the founder reply, the packaging. If the business is speaking, that is marketing. It is not a department. It is not ads.

Advertising is one kind of that communication. It is not the whole of it.

05 / DEFINE THE WORDS

A brand is the association someone has with something.

It lives in their head, not in your logo file. You do not ship a brand in a deck. People form it from every piece of communication — and from whether the thing you sold held up.

Branding is only the word for trying to influence that association.

06 / DEFINE THE WORDS

Advertising brings someone’s attention to something specific.

That is the job. A paid ad, a billboard, a launch post, a cold email with one object: look at this. It is marketing whose purpose is attention, not the relationship after.

Measured first with impressions — how many times was that specific thing put in front of someone?

Impressions start the scoreboard. They do not prove anyone cared.

07 / WHY THEY MOVE

People buy to solve a problem or fulfill a desire.

That is almost the whole game. Marketing cannot create a new desire inside someone. It can only tap a desire they already have, and cause them to act.

If the desire is not there, more reach is just more noise.

08 / THE VALUE EQUATION

The action they take is always an investment — even when no money moves.

A click, an email, a call, a purchase. Attention and time are currencies. You asked them to spend something.

Perceived value

(outcome × likelihood of achievement) (effort & sacrifice × time delay)

Frequency of similar claims divides the left side and lowers perceived value. That is market sophistication — a story for another day.

09 / THE SCOREBOARD

Every action is a signal of resonance. The metrics are the strength of that signal.

CTR> 1.5%
Opt-in / CVR> 2% ideally
Opt-in → customer> 10%
LTV : CAC> 3

Below the bar, the market is not answering. Do not scale the channel.

10 / THE CONSTRAINT

You do not have a tactics problem.

You have a first-principles problem.

  1. 01Fuzzy buyer → message cannot convert
  2. 02Unclear offer → traffic is expensive
  3. 03Guessed CTR → creative gets blamed for volume
  4. 04Unknown CAC → spend scales a leak
  5. 05Memory follow-up → intent dies cold
  6. 06Ignored LTV → acquisition is treated as the whole business

Often the copy asks for an investment the value equation cannot support, or speaks to a desire that is not there. Agencies get hired to add fuel. Nobody checks if the bucket holds water.
