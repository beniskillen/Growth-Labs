# First principles — sponsor ROI and valence

Sponsors do not buy logos. They buy a path from **attention they cannot buy themselves** to **a customer worth more than the cheque**.

## Valence scoreboard (Growth Labs governor)

Work left to right. Fix the broken shell only.

| Shell | Metric | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attention | Impressions | Are enough **right-fit** people seeing the offer? |
| Attention | Unique views | Is unique demand landing on the property? |
| Attention | CTR % | Does the message turn attention into intent? |
| Economics | Page / path CVR % | Does qualified intent become a customer or a booked call? |
| Economics | CAC | What does it actually cost to acquire? |
| Economics | LTV | What is the customer worth after the first purchase? |
| Bound | TAM | Outer potential — never a budget |

**Rule:** do not scale impressions while CVR is zero on a meaningful sample. Media value without a conversion mechanism sells the sponsor short and will not survive a procurement conversation.

## Sponsor chain (what they actually underwrite)

```
Reach (impressions)
  → Segments (who, geos, ICP mix)
    → Attribution (how we prove they were in the path)
      → Conversion (trial, activation, FTD, booked call, install)
        → LTV (vs their other acquisition channels)
          → ROI (why this cheque beats hiring a GTM rep or buying ads)
```

Different categories care about different links. Research the category. Do not pitch casino math to a developer-tools company.

| Category analogue | They cannot easily buy | They measure | Typical commercial shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Restricted / regulated (casino, some crypto) | Paid media in target geos | FTD / AFT, geo mix, CPA | Upfront for proven tracked acquisition; otherwise rev-share |
| CRM / automation (e.g. GoHighLevel) | Trusted operator rooms | Activations, accounts, usage | Category exclusivity + case study + software/in-kind |
| Dev tools / LLM (e.g. Cursor) | Documented adoption in real businesses | Activations, completed builds, qualified accounts | Hybrid cash + credits; they are hiring GTM — compete with that seat |
| Wellness / “divine” | Meaning, not lead volume | Depth of integration, retention | Smaller cash, longer relationship |
| Place / Bali / hospitality | Arrivals | Room nights, spend | Trade against F&B minimums, not MQLs |

## Custom first, tiers as downsell

Flagship sponsors get a **custom integration thesis**: agenda moments, coursework, credits, attribution, fulfilment after the event. Tiers are the natural downsell when they will not fund custom.

Do not open with a rate card. Rate cards sell what is convenient for the organiser (impressions, booths). Custom sells what is convenient for the sponsor (a job in the attendee journey).

One category winner. Category boundaries are set **before** exclusivity is granted. The **client** owns the attendee relationship. Vendors get only consented data required for delivery.

## Media value (honesty)

```
claimed impressions × niche CPM band = notional media value
```

Only put this in a deck when:

1. Impression sources are itemised (owned, partner, paid, hoped)
2. CPM band has a cited analogue
3. Earned vs paid vs hoped are separate columns

AI-adjacent consumer CPM is often cited in the $10–$30 range. Treat that as a **band to validate**, not a fact. 30 million impressions at $30 CPM is $900k **notional** — not cash, not booked, not geos-qualified.

Heritage proof (Juicy ticket/sponsor scale, CryptoVega ~$500k structure, Bybit/KOL, casino FTD language) is **Growth Labs method proof**. Never imply the current client already has those numbers.

## Structure to steal from engineered sponsorships (not the content)

From high-production sponsorship pitches (CryptoVega-class):

- ROI formula on page one
- Itemised impression map with geos
- Every placement named, dated, owned
- Event as the **start** of a campaign (clips, after-movie, follow-on events)
- Make-goods defined before sale

Never copy degenerate activation content into an education or operator deck. Steal the **engineering**, not the dunk tank.

## Deep-integration rules (copy onto every sponsor brief)

- Every placement has a job: activation, content, data, attendee utility, or pipeline.
- Cash, in-kind, and supplier spend are separate. Credits are not cash.
- Rights (recording, clips, replay, likeness, case studies) are explicit.
- Fulfilment cost (design, staff, reporting) sits in the margin.
- Renewal starts at sale (waitlist through post-event tail).
- No sponsor moment interrupts the client’s backend conversion.

## Constraint theory for this skill

Three possible bottlenecks: **leads, sales (conversion), fulfilment**.

Name one. Work only that one this cycle.

Worked example (AIP only — do not reuse the digits): 25k impressions, 113 unique views, 36 email clicks, 10 DMs, 0 booked calls was a **conversion** constraint. Impression theatre is malpractice until **this** path books. Pull the current funnel; name **this** bottleneck.
